# Testimonial Proof Systems Guide

## Purpose

- Use proof to reduce risk without making fake or contextless claims.

## Discovery Questions

- What proof is approved and attributable?
- What claim does each testimonial support?
- Can names, roles, logos, metrics, or context be shown?
- Where should proof appear in the decision path?

## Implementation Rules

- Do not invent testimonials, logos, ratings, or metrics.
- Give quotes enough context to be credible.
- Place proof near claims and objections.
- Avoid logo walls with no explanation for high-stakes pages.
- Use caveats or methodology for metrics.

## Useful Patterns

- Quote card: quote, name, role, company, context, result.
- Metric proof: number, baseline, timeframe, source/method.
- Logo wall with caption explaining relationship.
- Case-study excerpt linked to full story.

## Anti-Patterns

- 'This changed everything' - anonymous.
- Logo soup.
- Metrics with no baseline.
- Testimonials that all repeat the same vague praise.

## QA Checklist

- Verify approvals, attribution, and claims.
- Check review/structured data only reflects visible proof.
- Inspect mobile proof density.
- Remove weak or redundant quotes.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Proof is specific, credible, and placed where it helps users decide.
- No fake or unsupported social proof remains.
- Trust sections feel earned.
