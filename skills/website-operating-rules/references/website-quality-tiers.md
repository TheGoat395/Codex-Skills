# Premium website quality tiers

Portable reference for this public collection. Current user direction, applicable project requirements and accepted delivery scope control. If a separately installed `route-sellable-web-tier` specialist is adopted by the project, reconcile with it; its absence never blocks these criteria. These are adaptable delivery profiles, not automatic scope expansion or a mandatory animation quota.

Select a profile only when the requested delivery depth is unresolved; reuse an adequate existing brief. These tiers describe the required outcome, not the price of a library or the framework used.

## Tier 1 - Regular wow-effect

The lower high-end tier for a regular business modernization or “wow-effect” landing site. Viktor Oddy and MotionSites are useful live reference sources for cinematic hero concepts, motion hooks, generative imagery, and fast first-impression craft.

Required outcome:

- a distinctive first viewport and one coherent signature interaction;
- clear offer, audience, primary action, and truthful content;
- intentional typography, responsive composition, and art-directed media;
- real motion choreography rather than generic reveal spam;
- accessible focus, reduced-motion behavior, stable loading, and a performant fallback;
- complete mobile treatment and no template leftovers.

This tier can be visually impressive while remaining a focused marketing site. A hero demo alone does not pass.

## Tier 2 - Agency / SaaS commercial system

The mid tier for an agency, studio, SaaS, product, or service site that must sell, explain, and support ongoing content.

Everything in Tier 1, plus:

- complete information architecture and page-level conversion narrative;
- product or service proof, case-study structure, objections, comparison, and credible calls to action;
- reusable design tokens and responsive section/component systems;
- real product UI or source-faithful visual proof instead of decorative fake dashboards;
- CMS, forms, analytics, SEO, consent, and editor workflow as required;
- loading, error, empty, form-success, and failure states where applicable;
- accessibility, performance, cross-browser, and content QA;
- client ownership, maintainability, handoff, and support boundaries.

## Tier 3 - Enterprise design

The highest tier: the level associated with the strongest Framer, Webflow, independent-studio, and award-caliber enterprise launches, combined with enterprise delivery discipline. Framer or Webflow alone does not create this standard.

Everything in Tiers 1 and 2, plus:

- original brand and interaction direction that survives comparison with current leading work;
- multi-audience and multi-page information architecture with clear governance;
- deep proof systems, editorial/case-study templates, and scalable CMS schemas;
- localization, privacy, security, consent, governance, and integration requirements where applicable;
- defined browser/device floor, performance budgets, accessibility target, and measurement plan;
- robust media, 3D, shader, and motion degradation paths;
- production monitoring, analytics validation, redirects, metadata, structured data, and recovery;
- stakeholder review surfaces, documentation, client-owned accounts, maintainable implementation, and professional handoff;
- visual regression, mobile, cross-browser, accessibility, performance, functional, and launch QA with evidence.

Tier 3 must be visually memorable and operationally trustworthy. A cinematic surface without enterprise information, ownership, resilience, and QA remains Tier 1.

## Routing rule

When the user names a tier, preserve its agreed meaning. Otherwise treat the following as discovery hints and resolve actual deliverables from the brief:

- “Viktor Oddy,” “MotionSites,” “wow-effect modernization” -> Tier 1 baseline;
- Commercial agency/SaaS delivery with ongoing content or integrations -> consider Tier 2; price alone does not establish scope;
- “enterprise,” “best Framer/Webflow,” “flagship,” “top-tier award-level” -> Tier 3 baseline.

Higher tiers inherit all lower-tier requirements. A project may deliberately borrow a Tier 1 visual language while still requiring Tier 2 or Tier 3 delivery depth.
